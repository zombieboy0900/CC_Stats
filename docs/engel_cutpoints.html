Engel Cut Points

Where nine callosotomy papers draw the line between a favorable and an unfavorable outcome — and why most of them don't draw it on the Engel scale at all.

The scale being cut

  • IFree of disabling seizures
  • IIRare disabling seizures — “almost seizure-free”

Four ordered classes admit three binary splits. All three appear in the callosotomy literature — one paper each, which is the first thing worth knowing: there is no dominant Engel convention in this procedure.

Kanai et al. — Insufficient efficacy of corpus callosotomy for epileptic spasms with biphasic muscular contractions

Frontiers in Neurology · 2020 · n = 17

Dichotomized as spasm-free (Engel I, n = 7) against residual spasms (Engel II–IV, n = 10).

The strictest bar found in a callosotomy series, and the title reflects what that bar produces: applying the resective standard to a palliative procedure classes 10 of 17 patients as failures.

J Korean Neurosurg Soc · 2007 · n = 39 · pp. 16–19

“Satisfactory outcome (Engel's class I, II) was obtained in 20 patients (51%) of 39 patients. In 36 cases with drop attack seizures, the class I, II outcomes were 22 patients (61%).”

Long-term outcomes of corpus callosotomy in adult patients with drug-resistant epilepsy — single center, Mexico

2025 · n = 63

Favorable (Engel I–III): 44 patients (69.8%). Unfavorable (Engel IV A–B): 14 (22.2%). Worsening (Engel IV C): 5 (7.9%).

The one published precedent for this study's cut, and the closest match in design — adults, callosotomy, long-term follow-up. It also subdivides class IV to separate “no improvement” from “worse than baseline,” which a plain 1–4 engel_class column cannot express.

Complete corpus callosotomy brings worthwhile seizure reduction in both pediatric and adult patients

Neurosurgery · 2025 · Williamson's criteria

“The F, E, and G classes were considered to represent worthwhile improvements” — freedom, >80%, and 50–80% reduction, against poor (<50%).

Different scale, same boundary: the line falls at 50% reduction, which is where Engel III's lower edge is usually understood to sit.

≥50%

Chourasia et al. — Influence of extent and age at corpus callosotomy on seizure outcomes

Epilepsia Open · 2023 · n = 38

Outcomes defined as “≥50% reduction in drop seizures” and “complete freedom from drop seizures.” Engel appears only when citing other studies.

Why the boundary is soft

“Engel's category ‘worthwhile improvement’ is ambiguous and is therefore calculated or interpreted differently from center to center. Some centers, for example, ask for a ≥90% reduction; others accept ≥50% or ≥60% or ≥75%… the term ‘worthwhile’ requires a subjective judgment that may not be entirely justified, so this term is best avoided.”

Wieser et al., ILAE proposal for a new outcome classification, Epilepsia 2001  forPoster/Papers/New-Classification…2001 (1).pdf

The I–III vs IV split is the least reproducible of the three, because it depends on exactly the boundary the ILAE singled out as center-dependent. Graham et al.'s pediatric callosotomy systematic review (Epilepsia 2016) found the same from the other side, reporting that across twelve studies “there was very little agreement among authors on the definition of a good seizure outcome.”

This is also why an ILAE ≤4 vs 5 split is easier to defend than the Engel equivalent: the ILAE scale anchors its boundary to a stated ≥50% reduction rather than to a clinical judgment call.

The argument against cutting at all

“Relying on seizure freedom as a primary outcome amounts to comparing Engel I vs Engel II–IV. There are two additional dichotomous categorizations: Engel I–II vs Engel III–IV and Engel I–III vs Engel IV. Rather than fit three individual binary regressions, ordinal regression fits three separate intercepts and a common, pooled odds ratio.”

Dickey, Krafty & Pedersen, Epilepsia Open 2022;7:344–349  forPoster/Papers/EPI4-7-344.pdf

Across 239 patients from eight studies, their power to detect mesial temporal sclerosis as a predictor was 29% under ordinal regression against 13% under binary logistic — the same data, more than double the power, purely from not collapsing the scale.

With 50 subjects, ten seizure types, and nothing surviving false-discovery correction, that gap is not academic. Some of the null result is genuine absence of signal; some is power discarded at the cut.

What this means for the analysis

The cut is defensible but not conventional. One published callosotomy series uses Engel I–III vs IV, and it is the closest match to this cohort's design. That is enough to cite, and it is not enough to call standard — a reviewer who asks why seizure freedom wasn't used is asking a fair question.

The field's actual convention is a target-seizure endpoint. Six of the nine papers here judge callosotomy on drop-attack freedom or ≥50% reduction, including both large meta-analyses. A drop-attack or atonic-specific endpoint would sit closer to the literature than any whole-patient Engel dichotomy.

Reporting the distribution costs nothing. Two of the nine report all four Engel classes without collapsing them. Publishing the 5 / 9 / 25 / 11 breakdown alongside the binary lets a reader apply whichever cut they prefer, and pre-empts the objection that the cut was chosen after seeing the results.

Scope and verification. Corpus callosotomy series and callosotomy meta-analyses only; resective and VNS papers excluded. Park et al. (Ann Child Neurol 2023) was dropped despite involving callosotomy patients, because its Engel I–II cut grades the resective surgery that followed, not the callosotomy.
